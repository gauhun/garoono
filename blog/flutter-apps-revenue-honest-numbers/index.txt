<h2>What worked</h2>
<h3>One app found a real job to do</h3>
<p>XLSheetAI is an AI helper for spreadsheets<br>People open it when they&#39;re stuck on a formula at work<br>That&#39;s a problem with money attached, and it&#39;s the only app in my portfolio that earns something every month</p>
<h3>Retention can be built</h3>
<p>JapMala, a prayer bead counter, keeps about 1 in 3 users on day one and about 1 in 5 into week two<br>That&#39;s my best retention by far</p>
<p>It earns almost nothing<br>But it proved I can make an app people come back to on their own</p>
<h3>Flutter was never the problem</h3>
<p>One codebase let me ship 16 apps on two stores as a solo dev<br>No regrets there<br>Every problem below is a business problem, not a tech one</p>
<h2>What didn&#39;t work</h2>
<h3>Counting installs instead of buyers</h3>
<p>I chased downloads for years<br>Most of mine came from Android and from India<br>Those users are real, and I like building for them<br>But in my apps, one new iOS user is worth about 12 new Android users</p>
<p>It&#39;s not just me<br>RevenueCat&#39;s State of Subscription Apps 2026 puts median 14 day revenue per install at $0.38 in North America and $0.08 in India and Southeast Asia (<a href="https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">RevenueCat</a>)</p>
<h3>Shipping wide instead of deep</h3>
<p>16 apps means 16 paywalls, 16 store listings and 16 sets of reviews to answer<br>Most of mine got one launch and then nothing<br>Eight of them make close to zero</p>
<h3>Trusting ads to fill the gap</h3>
<p>I added interstitial and rewarded ads to pay for free users<br>They loaded fine, then they were shown about once in every 50 loads</p>
<p>So almost all my ad money came from small banners<br>And most of my ad traffic came from India, where my eCPM was about $0.16<br>In the US it was about $12.39</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Ads don&#39;t pay when they don&#39;t show, and when they do show, they pay by where your users live</p>
</blockquote>
<h3>A paywall that loses buyers at the very last step</h3>
<p>In XLSheetAI, 1,109 people saw the paywall in 30 days<br>275 tapped buy<br>About 26 finished</p>
<p>Roughly 5 out of every 6 of those purchase starts were cancelled on Apple&#39;s or Google&#39;s own payment sheet<br>These people wanted it enough to tap the button<br>Then something on that last screen changed their mind<br>I&#39;m writing that funnel up in detail next</p>
<h3>Flying blind for a while</h3>
<p>In PushPass, the PostHog key came from <code>--dart-define</code> only<br>From <code>v1.2.2</code> on, builds went out without it, <code>String.fromEnvironment</code> returned an empty string, and the SDK quietly switched itself off<br>Every analytics event after that was lost</p>
<p>Now release builds fall back to a bundled <code>.env</code> and shout when a key is missing:</p>
<pre><code class="language-dart">import &#39;package:flutter/foundation.dart&#39;;
import &#39;package:flutter_dotenv/flutter_dotenv.dart&#39;;

const _fromDefine = String.fromEnvironment(&#39;POSTHOG_KEY&#39;);

String posthogKey() {
  final key = _fromDefine.isNotEmpty
      ? _fromDefine
      : (dotenv.env[&#39;POSTHOG_KEY&#39;] ?? &#39;&#39;);
  if (key.isEmpty) debugPrint(&#39;POSTHOG_KEY MISSING: analytics is OFF&#39;);
  return key;
}
</code></pre>
<p>If you take one thing from this post, take that<br>You can&#39;t fix a funnel you can&#39;t see</p>
<h2>What I&#39;m changing next</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>iOS first</strong>, new features and new apps ship on iOS first, and Android follows once the iOS version earns</li>
<li><strong>Tier 1 markets</strong>, listings, screenshots and tests aimed at the US, UK and Europe, instead of wherever installs happen to come from</li>
<li><strong>Fix the one paywall that matters</strong>, XLSheetAI gets a free trial on the yearly plan, a clearer price on the paywall itself and a cheaper intro offer, before I touch any other app</li>
<li><strong>Freeze the tail</strong>, Fitess, Forma, Repvolve, JSON View, NailMirror, Focuson, SparkMate and Habitide stay live, but get bug fixes only</li>
</ul>
<p>Number 4 is the hard one<br>Each of those apps was a weekend I was proud of<br>But 16 half-loved apps lost to one app that people actually need</p>
<h2>Quick answers</h2>
<h3>Should Flutter devs skip Android?</h3>
<p>No<br>Android still brought most of my users and some of my revenue<br>One codebase means it costs almost nothing extra<br>I just stop building for it first</p>
<h3>Are ads worth it in a small Flutter app?</h3>
<p>Only if full-screen ads actually show, and only if your users live in markets that pay<br>Check the show rate in AdMob, not just the match rate</p>
<h3>Why freeze apps instead of deleting them?</h3>
<p>Live apps still pick up a few installs and cost me almost nothing to keep<br>Freezing stops the time drain without throwing anything away</p>
<h3>What would you do differently from day one?</h3>
<p>Put events on the paywall before launch: paywall view, purchase start, purchase cancelled, purchase success<br>Launch on iOS<br>Build one app deep before starting a second</p>
<h2>The takeaway</h2>
<p>100,000 downloads didn&#39;t make me a business<br>One app with a real job, users in markets that pay and a paywall that doesn&#39;t scare people at the last step might</p>
<p>Next goal: $500 MRR, mostly from iOS<br>I&#39;ll post the honest numbers again when I get there, or when I don&#39;t</p>
