20:T2042,<p>From today, some Android phones will refuse to install apps from developers Google has not verified<br>Next year that goes global, India included<br>If you only publish on Google Play, you are probably fine, but &quot;probably&quot; is not a plan</p>
<p>Here is what changed, what you actually need to do, and the one mistake that can lock you out of your own app</p>
<h2>What changed on September 30, 2026</h2>
<p>Google now requires apps to be registered by verified developers before they can be installed or updated on certified Android devices in Brazil, Indonesia, Singapore and Thailand (<a href="https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/03/android-developer-verification-rolling-out-to-all-developers.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Android Developers Blog</a>)<br>Google says it will roll the requirement out globally from 2027 (<a href="https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/03/android-developer-verification-rolling-out-to-all-developers.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Android Developers Blog</a>)</p>
<p>For now it covers installs from the participating stores, Google Play among them<br>Apps sideloaded directly or shipped through other stores are not affected yet, but Google recommends verifying before the global rollout (<a href="https://developer.android.com/developer-verification/guides/faq" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Android developer verification FAQ</a>)</p>

<h2>Do you need to do anything?</h2>
<h3>If you publish only on Google Play</h3>
<p>Google says that if you have completed Play Console&#39;s developer verification, your identity is already verified and eligible Play apps are registered for you automatically (<a href="https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/03/android-developer-verification-rolling-out-to-all-developers.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Android Developers Blog</a>)<br>Open Play Console, confirm your account verification is complete, and check that each app shows as registered<br>That is a two minute job, do it today</p>
<h3>If you use Play App Signing</h3>
<p>Good news, Google says apps using Play App Signing are part of the automatic registration (<a href="https://developer.android.com/developer-verification/guides/faq" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Android developer verification FAQ</a>)</p>
<h3>If you share APKs outside Play</h3>
<p>This is where indie devs get caught<br>Direct APK downloads from your website, betas shared on Telegram, or builds on other stores all need a verified developer once the global rollout reaches your users</p>
<blockquote>
<p>The mistake that locks you out: Google says if you lose your signing key, you won&#39;t be able to register your packages (<a href="https://developer.android.com/developer-verification/guides/faq" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Android developer verification FAQ</a>)</p>
</blockquote>
<p>Back up your signing and upload keys somewhere safe tonight<br>If an old app was never enrolled in Play App Signing, check where its key lives before 2027</p>

<h2>What developers in India should do now</h2>
<p>India is not in the first four countries, so nothing changes for your users this year<br>It is part of the 2027 global rollout, so use the quiet months to get ready</p>
<p>For individual developers in India, Google Play accepts an Indian passport, driving licence, voter ID or PAN card as photo ID (<a href="https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/15633622?hl=en&co=GENIE.CountryCode%3DIN" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Google Play Help</a>)<br>Your proof of address has to show your name and address exactly as they appear on your developer profile (<a href="https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/15633622?hl=en&co=GENIE.CountryCode%3DIN" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Google Play Help</a>)<br>A mismatch between the name on your PAN and your profile name is an easy way to get stuck, so fix that first</p>
<h2>Can I still test and sideload?</h2>
<p>Yes, for development<br>Google says developers are free to install apps without verification using ADB (<a href="https://developer.android.com/developer-verification/guides/faq" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Android developer verification FAQ</a>)<br>For a handful of real testers outside Play, the free limited distribution account covers up to 20 devices (<a href="https://support.google.com/android-developer-console/answer/16561738?hl=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Android Developer Console Help</a>)</p>
<h2>Why so many developers are angry</h2>
<p>Google frames this as anti malware, citing an analysis of apps from internet sideloaded sources<br>F-Droid says it has not seen that analysis and calls developer verification an existential threat to free software distribution platforms like F-Droid and to emerging Play Store competitors (<a href="https://f-droid.org/en/2025/10/28/sideloading.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">F-Droid</a>)</p>
<p>My take as someone shipping on Play: for Play only indies this is mostly paperwork<br>The real cost lands on people who distribute outside Play, and on anyone who lost a signing key years ago</p>
<h2>Your checklist before 2027</h2>
<ul>
<li>Confirm Play Console developer verification is complete</li>
<li>Check every app shows as registered</li>
<li>Back up signing and upload keys in two places</li>
<li>Enroll old apps in Play App Signing where possible</li>
<li>Match your profile name and address to your ID and address proof</li>
<li>Decide your account type if you ship APKs outside Play</li>
<li>Start a D-U-N-S request early if you publish as a company</li>
</ul>
