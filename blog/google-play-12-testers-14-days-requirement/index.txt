2e:T2ed0,<p>If you made a personal Google Play developer account after November 13, 2023, you can&#39;t go straight to production<br>You first run a closed test with at least 12 testers who stay opted in for at least 14 days in a row (<a href="https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465?hl=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Play Console Help</a>)<br>Then you apply for production access from the Play Console Dashboard and answer questions about that test<br>Google reviews your answers, and if the test looks thin, it asks you to keep testing</p>
<p>Finding 12 names is the easy part<br>Keeping 12 real people opted in, and showing they actually used the app, is where solo devs lose weeks<br>Here&#39;s the exact rule, what counts, why access gets refused, and a plan that fits into 1 to 2 hours a night</p>
<h2>Who the rule applies to</h2>
<p>The testing requirement covers personal developer accounts created after November 13, 2023 (<a href="https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6112435?hl=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Play Console Help</a>)<br>Google&#39;s account setup page labels that section &quot;Personal accounts only&quot; (<a href="https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6112435?hl=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Play Console Help</a>)</p>
<p>So three cases:</p>

<p>Until you get production access, Production and Pre-registration stay locked in Play Console (<a href="https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465?hl=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Play Console Help</a>)</p>
<h2>The exact rule, in Google&#39;s words</h2>
<p>Google asks for a closed test with &quot;a minimum of 12 testers who have been opted in continuously for at least 14 days&quot; (<a href="https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465?hl=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Play Console Help</a>)</p>
<p>The word that trips people up is continuously<br>Google&#39;s FAQ says testers who opt in, test for fewer than 14 days and then opt out don&#39;t count (<a href="https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465?hl=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Play Console Help</a>)<br>And if a tester opts out and later opts back in, only a fresh run of 14 consecutive days counts</p>
<p>So 15 testers who drift in and out can still leave you short<br>12 who join on day one and never leave is a pass on the numbers</p>

<h2>What counts as an opted in tester</h2>
<p>Adding an email to a list is not the same as having a tester<br>Each tester has to open your opt-in link and accept it (<a href="https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9845334?hl=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Play Console Help</a>)</p>
<p>A few details from Google&#39;s testing setup page:</p>
<ul>
<li>You add testers by email list or by Google Group</li>
<li>Testers need a Google Account or a Google Workspace account</li>
<li>With a Google Group, people must join the group before they can opt in</li>
<li>The opt-in link only shows once the closed track status is Published</li>
<li>After your first publish, the link can take several hours to work for testers</li>
</ul>
<p>All of that is on the same <a href="https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9845334?hl=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Play Console Help page</a></p>
<blockquote>
<p>A tester only counts after they tap your opt-in link and accept, and only while they stay opted in</p>
</blockquote>
<p>My advice: ask each tester to send you a screenshot of the &quot;you&#39;re a tester&quot; screen<br>It takes them ten seconds and it saves you guessing on day 14</p>
<h2>Why production access gets refused</h2>
<p>Google names two reasons for asking you to keep testing: fewer than 12 opted in testers, or not enough tester engagement (<a href="https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465?hl=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Play Console Help</a>)</p>
<p>The first is just counting<br>The second is where the form matters</p>
<p>When you apply, Google asks three groups of questions (<a href="https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465?hl=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Play Console Help</a>):</p>
<ul>
<li>About your closed test: how easy recruiting was, whether testers used all the features, how usage compared to what you expect in production, and a summary of the feedback and how you collected it</li>
<li>About your app: who it&#39;s for, why it&#39;s useful, and how many installs you expect in year one</li>
<li>About production readiness: what you changed because of the test, and how you decided it was ready</li>
</ul>
<p>Read that list again<br>Google wants evidence that people used the app and that you acted on what they said<br>A test where 12 strangers installed once and never opened it again gives you nothing to write</p>
<p>That&#39;s why I&#39;m wary of tester swap groups and paid tester packages<br>They can get you to 12, but they rarely give you feedback worth putting in the form</p>
<h2>A plan that fits after work</h2>
<h3>Before day 0, line up 15 to 20 people</h3>
<p>Ask more than 12 so one or two dropping out doesn&#39;t reset you<br>Start with people who would use the app anyway: friends, coworkers, family, people who asked you about the idea<br>If you already have apps live, a short note to those users can find people who like your work<br>The same channels I use to find <a href="/blog/how-to-get-your-first-100-app-users/">the first 100 app users</a> work here too</p>
<h3>Day 0, publish the closed track and send the link</h3>
<p>Use a Google Group, it&#39;s easier to manage than a list of emails<br>Send one message with the group link, the opt-in link, and what to try first<br>Collect the opt-in screenshots the same evening</p>
<h3>Days 1 to 14, give testers a reason to open it</h3>
<p>Set up a feedback channel, Google suggests email, a website or a messaging group<br>Ask for one small thing every few days, like trying a single feature<br>Ship at least one update during the test, Google itself recommends updating your app in closed testing before production<br>Keep a simple note of every piece of feedback and what you changed</p>
<h3>Day 15, apply with real answers</h3>
<p>Count opted in testers in Play Console before you click Apply for production<br>Fill the form from your notes, name the features people used and the fixes you shipped<br>Then wait, Google says review usually takes seven days or less (<a href="https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465?hl=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Play Console Help</a>)</p>
<h2>Quick checklist</h2>
<ul>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> Personal account created after November 13, 2023, so the rule applies</li>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> 15 to 20 people lined up before you publish the closed track</li>

<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> Nobody opted out for 14 days in a row</li>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> A feedback channel testers actually use</li>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> At least one update shipped during the test</li>
<li><input disabled="" type="checkbox"> Form answers written from real feedback, not guesses</li>
</ul>

<h2>FAQ</h2>
<h3>Does the 12 testers rule apply to organization accounts?</h3>
<p>Google&#39;s rule covers personal developer accounts created after November 13, 2023<br>Organization accounts are not covered by it as Google writes it</p>
<h3>What happens if a tester opts out before 14 days?</h3>
<p>That tester stops counting toward the requirement<br>If they opt back in, they need a new run of 14 consecutive days to count</p>
<h3>Do testers have to install the app or just join the list?</h3>
<p>Each tester has to accept your opt-in link, being on the email list or Google Group is not enough<br>Google also checks engagement, so testers should install the app and use it</p>
<h3>Can I use paid testers or tester exchange groups?</h3>
<p>They can get you to 12 opted in testers<br>Google asks about tester engagement and feedback when you apply, so testers who never use the app make it harder to get access</p>
<h3>How long does the production access review take?</h3>
<p>Google says review usually takes seven days or less, and occasionally longer<br>If Google asks you to keep testing, run the closed test longer and apply again</p>
