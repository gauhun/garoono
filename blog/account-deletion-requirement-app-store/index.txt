2e:T37af,<p>If people can create an account in your app, both stores want them to be able to delete it from inside the app<br>Apple checks it under guideline 5.1.1(v), Google checks it through its User Data policy and the Data safety form<br>Deactivating the account does not count on either store<br>Deleting must remove the account and the data tied to it, and Google also wants a web link that works without the app</p>
<p>That&#39;s the whole rule<br>The rest of this post is how to build it once, properly, and how to prove it to App Review</p>
<p>In October 2026 one of my apps got rejected under 5.1.1(v) for exactly this<br>It had sign-in and no in-app account deletion<br>After I added it, Apple asked for a screen recording on a physical device showing the full flow<br>So this is written from the fix, not from theory</p>
<h2>What Apple requires</h2>
<p>Guideline 5.1.1(v) says that if your app supports account creation, you must also offer account deletion within the app (<a href="https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#data-collection-and-storage" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">App Review Guidelines</a>)</p>
<p>Apple&#39;s account deletion page spells out the details (<a href="https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/guideline-reference/5-1-1-account-deletion" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Apple</a>)</p>
<ul>
<li>Make the option easy to find, usually in account settings</li>
<li>Delete the entire account record and the personal data tied to it</li>
<li>Only offering to deactivate or disable the account is not enough</li>
<li>If deletion finishes on your website, link straight to that page, not your home page</li>
<li>If deletion takes time, tell the user how long and confirm when it&#39;s done</li>
<li>If the law makes you keep some data, say what you keep and what you delete</li>
</ul>
<p>Two details catch indie devs</p>
<p>Guest accounts count<br>Apple says people should be able to delete automatically generated accounts and their data too (<a href="https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/guideline-reference/5-1-1-account-deletion" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Apple</a>)</p>
<p>Subscriptions need a warning<br>If the user has an auto-renewable subscription, Apple wants you to tell them billing continues through Apple and ask them to cancel first (<a href="https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/guideline-reference/5-1-1-account-deletion" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Apple</a>)</p>
<p>You may ask the user to confirm, or enter a code sent to their email<br>But Apple says apps that make deletion unnecessarily difficult will not pass review, and unless you&#39;re in a highly regulated industry, don&#39;t make people call, email or chat with support to delete (<a href="https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/guideline-reference/5-1-1-account-deletion" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Apple</a>)</p>
<h2>What Google Play requires</h2>
<p>Google&#39;s rule is close, with one extra piece</p>
<p>Its User Data policy says apps that allow account creation must offer deletion both inside the app and through a web resource outside it (<a href="https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Google Play policy</a>)<br>The same policy says temporary deactivation, disabling or freezing the account does not qualify as deletion</p>
<p>Google&#39;s help page on the requirement adds what the web link needs (<a href="https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Google Play Help</a>)</p>
<ul>
<li>It must load without errors</li>
<li>It must reference your app or developer name as shown on your Play listing</li>
<li>The user must be able to actually request deletion through it</li>
<li>You put the link in the Data safety form in Play Console</li>
</ul>
<p>Why a web link if the app already has a button?<br>Google&#39;s answer is that a user who already uninstalled your app shouldn&#39;t have to download it again just to delete their account (<a href="https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Google Play Help</a>)</p>
<p>You can keep some data for security, fraud prevention or legal reasons, as long as you tell users clearly what you keep (<a href="https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Google Play Help</a>)</p>
<p>A simple page on your site with a short form, or a page that tells signed in users to tap Delete account and logged out users how to request it, is enough for a small app</p>

<p>The button is the easy part<br>What happens after the tap is what makes it real</p>

<h3>1 Confirm and warn</h3>
<p>One screen, one clear button, and a line about active subscriptions with a link to manage them</p>
<h3>2 Re-authenticate</h3>
<p>Firebase says deleting an account is a security-sensitive action that needs a recent sign-in, and fails with an error otherwise (<a href="https://firebase.google.com/docs/auth/web/manage-users" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Firebase docs</a>)<br>So ask the user to sign in again right before deleting, instead of waiting for the error</p>
<h3>3 Revoke Sign in with Apple</h3>
<p>Apple says apps that support Sign in with Apple should use its REST API to revoke user tokens (<a href="https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/guideline-reference/5-1-1-account-deletion" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Apple</a>)<br>Firebase does not store Apple tokens, so the user signs in with Apple again, you take the fresh authorization code, and Firebase revokes it for you (<a href="https://firebase.google.com/docs/auth/ios/apple" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Firebase docs</a>)</p>
<h3>4 Delete the server data</h3>
<p>Firestore documents, Storage files, anything the user posted<br>Apple says people expect shared content like photos, posts and reviews to go too (<a href="https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/guideline-reference/5-1-1-account-deletion" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Apple</a>)</p>
<h3>5 Delete the auth user</h3>
<p>Last, because your security rules probably check <code>request.auth</code> before letting anyone delete those documents</p>
<p>Here&#39;s the short Flutter version for an Apple sign-in user</p>
<pre><code class="language-dart">Future&lt;void&gt; deleteAccount() async {
  final auth = FirebaseAuth.instance;
  final user = auth.currentUser!;

  // Fresh sign-in: satisfies recent-login and gives a new Apple auth code
  final cred = await user.reauthenticateWithProvider(AppleAuthProvider());
  final code = cred.additionalUserInfo?.authorizationCode;
  if (code != null) await auth.revokeTokenWithAuthorizationCode(code);

  // Server data before the auth user, while rules still see request.auth
  await FirebaseFirestore.instance.doc(&#39;users/${user.uid}&#39;).delete();

  await user.delete();
}
</code></pre>
<p>The <code>revokeTokenWithAuthorizationCode</code> call is straight from the FlutterFire docs (<a href="https://firebase.google.com/docs/auth/flutter/federated-auth" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Firebase docs</a>)<br>For Google or email users, swap the reauthenticate step for that provider and skip the revoke</p>
<p>If a user has subcollections or Storage files, one document delete is not enough<br>Move the data part into a Cloud Function that deletes everything under the user&#39;s ID, and call it from the app</p>
<h2>How to show it to App Review</h2>
<p>This is the part Apple came back to me about</p>
<p>Apple&#39;s review prep page says to give working demo account credentials in App Store Connect, and to use the Notes field for anything extra reviewers need (<a href="https://developer.apple.com/help/app-review/before-submitting-for-review/complete-review" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Apple</a>)<br>For account deletion, add these to Review Notes before Apple asks</p>
<ul>
<li>A demo account that is safe to delete, and a second one in case they delete the first</li>
<li>The exact path, for example Settings, Account, Delete account</li>
<li>What gets deleted and how long it takes</li>
<li>A screen recording on a real iPhone: sign in, find Delete account, finish the deletion</li>
</ul>
<p>In my case Apple asked for that recording after the rejection<br>Recording it up front costs five minutes and can save you a full review cycle</p>
<blockquote>
<p>Deleting the account, not hiding it, is the rule on both stores</p>
</blockquote>

<h2>FAQ</h2>
<h3>Is account deletion required on the App Store?</h3>
<p>Yes, if your app lets people create an account<br>Guideline 5.1.1(v) requires that those apps also let people delete the account from inside the app</p>
<h3>Is deactivating an account enough?</h3>
<p>No<br>Apple and Google both say that only deactivating, disabling or freezing an account does not count as deletion</p>
<h3>Does Google Play need a web link for account deletion?</h3>
<p>Yes<br>Google wants a working web page where users can request deletion without reinstalling the app, and that link goes in the Data safety form in Play Console</p>
<h3>Do I have to revoke Sign in with Apple tokens when deleting an account?</h3>
<p>Apple says apps that support Sign in with Apple should revoke user tokens through its REST API<br>With Firebase, sign the user in with Apple again and pass the new authorization code to the revoke method before deleting the user</p>
<h3>Can I keep some user data after the account is deleted?</h3>
<p>Yes, if the law or fraud prevention requires it<br>Both stores want you to tell users clearly what you keep</p>
